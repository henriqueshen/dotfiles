# Banlist of writing patterns

This list names the words, constructions, and formatting habits that mark text as machine-written. The first part is specific to Claude models. The second part is the generic list that applies to every model. Check every response and every artifact against both before sending. When a banned item appears, rewrite the sentence so that it states the fact plainly. Do not replace one item on the list with another.

The list is compiled from community catalogues and from direct observation. Sources are at the end.

## Part 1. Patterns specific to Claude models

### Acknowledgement openers and self-narration

Never open with an evaluation of the other person's message or a report of your own state. Start with the content. Concede by stating the corrected fact, never the concession.

- "You're absolutely right", "You're right", "Fair point", "Both fair", "Good catch", "Great question", "Correct, and", "Exactly", "That's a great point"
- "I see the issue now", "I understand now", "Let me try a different approach", "Let me take a step back", "Upon further reflection", "On reflection"
- "Perfect!", "Great!", "Done!", "Excellent!", "I've successfully", "I've now", "I've gone ahead and"
- "I apologize for the confusion", "I apologize for my mistake"
- "Let me check", "Let me verify", "Let me look at", as narration of steps in the final message

### Candor and significance flags

Never announce that something is honest, genuine, real, key, or important. State the thing.

- "the honest answer", "honestly", "to be honest", "I'll be honest", "I want to be direct", "candidly"
- "genuinely", "genuine", "real" as an intensifier ("the real question", "the real problem", "the real work"), "actually", "what actually", "what's actually happening"
- "the key insight", "the key point", "the insight here", "the takeaway", "key takeaways", "the bottom line"
- "this matters because", "and that matters", "here's why it matters", "the point is", "the point here is"
- "crucially", "critically", "importantly", "notably", "interestingly", "significantly"
- "it's worth noting", "worth noting", "worth asking", "worth a look", "worth sitting with", "worth considering", "worth exploring", and every other "worth ___" construction
- "the tell", "that's the tell", "load-bearing", "the gap is", "the gap between", "that's the reframe", "the shift is", "the unlock", "what moved", "what changed is"
- "the crux", "the core issue", "at its core", "at the end of the day", "the whole game", "that's the whole game"
- "settled", "isn't settled", "an open question" used as drama rather than as a fact about a decision

### Contrastive binaries and reframes

State the positive claim alone.

- "not X but Y", "not X, Y", "not X. Y.", "It's not X, it's Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y", "X, not Y" as a slogan
- "not just X but Y", "not only X but also Y", "more than X, it's Y"
- "less about X than Y", "the question isn't X, it's Y", "the problem isn't X"
- "Better posed:", "Reframed:", "Put differently", "In other words", "That is to say", "To put it another way"
- "what changes is X, not Y", "the difference is X, not Y"
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."

### Abstractions as actors

Abstractions do not act. Use "is", "has", "causes", "requires", "produces", "means".

- "lives" ("the risk lives in"), "sits" ("the problem sits between"), "sits with"
- "carries", "holds", "hands", "hands you", "lands", "hits", "hits hardest", "lands hardest"
- "moves", "points", "points toward", "surfaces" as a verb, "names" as a verb ("names the problem")
- "compounds", "turns on" ("it all turns on"), "reaches for", "rides on", "rides along"
- "does the work", "does the heavy lifting", "is doing the lifting", "carries the argument", "falls out of"
- "quietly" as an adverb about change ("quietly rewrote", "quietly assumes")
- "drives", "shapes everything that follows", "unlocks", "collapses into"

### Copula avoidance

Write "is" and "are".

- "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents", "functions as", "acts as", "operates as", "boasts", "features", "offers"

### Rhetorical machinery

- Aphoristic closing lines that restate the point ("a stance, not its absence")
- Deflating tails ("and no more", "and nothing else", "that is all")
- Confidence by litotes ("not difficult to", "not inconsistent", "not wrong to")
- Corrective pivots ("It would be wrong, though, to", "That said,", "To be fair,")
- Anticipating and rebutting an objection nobody raised ("One might object that")
- Suspense hooks and colon reveals ("has a name:", "comes down to one thing:", "the answer is simple:")
- Meta-signposting ("three things", "two points", "four caveats belong up front", "I'm going to", "Let me explain", "Let's break this down", "Let's explore", "Let's unpack")
- Self-ranking ("the most important", "the largest change", "the deepest point", "the biggest risk", "the single most")
- Validate then promise precision ("is right, and it can be made precise", "is correct, with one refinement")
- Reflexive hedging as a verbal reflex ("almost", "tends to", "arguably", "in some sense", "to some extent", "it depends")
- "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "Here's where it gets interesting", "Here's what I'd do", "Here's the catch", "The catch is"
- "Think of it as", "Think of it like", "Imagine a world where", "Picture this"
- "The X? A Y." ("The result? Devastating.")
- Runs of staccato fragments (four or more short declaratives in a row), anaphora runs (three sentences opening the same way), sentences of uniform length
- False vulnerability ("And yes, I'm biased", "This is not a rant, it's a diagnosis")
- "The truth is", "The reality is simpler", "History is clear on this"
- Invented concept labels, that is, coining a name for an idea and reusing it ("the supervision paradox", "the acceleration trap")
- "we've seen this movie before", "we've been here before", "same story"
- "the first wave of", "a multi-year transition", "paradigm shift", "north star", "pressure-test", "right-size", "double-click on", "lean into", "unpack", "leverage" as a verb

### Invented observation

No claims of experience or of a crowd.

- "most teams I've seen", "everyone I've worked with", "in my experience", "the people I've watched", "a lot of folks", "nobody I know", "what I keep running into", "what I keep noticing"
- "most people", "many engineers", "teams often", used to attribute a view to an unnamed group

### Formatting

- Bold-first bullets in the "**Term:** explanation" shape, and bolded lead-ins to paragraphs or bullets
- Bullets that carry sentence fragments instead of sentences, or that split one line of argument into a list
- Headers in short messages, headers that contain only headers, title case in headings, level-one headings inside a document body
- Unicode arrows (→), curly quotes, checkmarks and other emoji, horizontal rules as section dividers
- Tables for non-tabular data
- Section summaries that restate what follows or what precedes, fractal summaries at every level, a closing paragraph that restates the thesis
- "Summary of changes", "Key changes", "What I did", and other sign-off summaries in a message that already said what was done
- Closing offers ("Let me know if", "Would you like me to", "Happy to")

## Part 2. Generic machine-writing patterns

### Vocabulary

delve, dive into, deep dive, navigate (figurative), landscape (figurative), tapestry, realm, testament, underscore, bolster, foster, harness, leverage, unpack, shed light on, pave the way, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changing, innovative, robust, comprehensive, seamless, intricate, nuanced, vibrant, multifaceted, holistic, elegant, powerful, exciting, straightforward, simply, just, easily, merely, ensure, streamline, empower, journey, embark, elevate, synergy, ecosystem (figurative), synthesize, enigmatic

### Phrases

- "It's important to note", "It cannot be overstated", "It goes without saying"
- "In today's rapidly evolving", "In the ever-changing", "When it comes to", "In the world of"
- "This is where X comes in", "Enter X", "Look no further"
- "A testament to", "reflecting a broader trend", "marking a significant shift", "plays a pivotal role"
- "Studies show", "experts say", "industry reports", "observers note", with no named source
- "Ranging from X to Y" false ranges
- "Despite its challenges", "Despite these challenges, X continues to"
- "In conclusion", "To sum up", "In summary", "Overall"

### Structures

- Rule-of-three lists and tricolons where two or five items would be accurate
- "And" and "But" as rhythmic sentence starters in runs
- Parenthetical asides stacked in one sentence, more than one dash-aside per sentence
- Hedging that presents every side as equal when one is stronger, and conclusions that decline to conclude
- "Despite X, faces challenges" outline paragraphs, "Challenges" and "Future outlook" sections
- Historical analogy stacking ("Apple didn't build Uber. Facebook didn't build Spotify.")
- One point restated in eight ways, dead metaphors repeated across a document
- Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, "as an AI" framing, collaborative "we'll explore" address

### Punctuation

- Em dashes, in any position
- Semicolons joining two sentences
- Colons in the "label: payload" shape, or in two consecutive sentences
